Great question — and this is one of those things where Portuguese (especially European vs Brazilian) leaks into conjugation tables and confuses learners.

### Short answer:
- **“havemos”** = the *standard, modern* conjugation of **haver** for **nós**
- **“hemos”** = an **archaic / literary / regional variant**, mostly from **European Portuguese** and older usage

---

### Let’s break it down

#### ✅ Modern standard (what you should use)
In both **Brazilian Portuguese** and modern standard usage:

| Pronoun | Verb |
|--------|------|
| nós | **havemos** |

Example:
- **Nós havemos de resolver isso.**  
(We shall / we have to solve this.)

---

#### ⚠️ “hemos” — what is it?

- It’s a **contracted / historical form** of *havemos*
- Mostly seen in:
  - **old texts**
  - **literary style**
  - occasionally **European Portuguese (Portugal)**

Example:
- **Nós hemos de vencer.** (sounds archaic / poetic)

### Important practical takeaway (for you)

Since you're learning **Brazilian Portuguese**:

👉 **Ignore “hemos” completely**  
👉 Always use **havemos**

---

### Bonus insight (this matters more than you think)

In **Brazilian Portuguese**, *haver* is **rarely used with “nós” at all** in everyday speech.

Instead, people prefer:
- **a gente tem que...** (we have to...)
- **nós temos que...** (more formal)

Example:
- Instead of: *nós havemos de ir*  
- Brazilians say: **a gente vai / a gente tem que ir**

---

### Bottom line

- **havemos** → correct, modern, safe  
- **hemos** → archaic, ignore for Brazil
