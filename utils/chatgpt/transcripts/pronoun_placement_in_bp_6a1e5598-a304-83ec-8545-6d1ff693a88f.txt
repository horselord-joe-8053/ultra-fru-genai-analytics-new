Your summary is essentially correct, but I would tighten it into a linguistic principle that explains most of modern Brazilian Portuguese:

# The Core Rule

Modern Brazilian Portuguese (BP) has largely shifted from a **clitic-placement system** to a **pronoun-before-verb system**.

Historically, Portuguese had a complex system of:

- **proclisis** → pronoun before verb
- **enclisis** → pronoun after verb
- **mesoclisis** → pronoun inside future/conditional forms

European Portuguese (EP) still preserves much of this system.

Brazilian Portuguese strongly favors:

> **Pronoun + Verb**

even where traditional grammar would prescribe enclisis.

---

# The Pattern

## European Portuguese

The default tends to be:

> Verb + Pronoun

Examples:

- Vi-te. ("I saw you.")
- Disse-lhe. ("I told him.")
- Chamaram-nos. ("They called us.")
- Olha-me. ("Look at me.")

---

## Brazilian Portuguese

The default tends to be:

> Pronoun + Verb

Examples:

- Te vi.
- Lhe disse.
- Nos chamaram.
- Me olha.

This happens even when traditional grammar says there is no proclisis trigger.

That is the key innovation.

---

# Why This Matters

Traditional Portuguese grammar teaches:

| Trigger present? | Classical rule |
|------------------|---------------|
| No trigger | Enclisis |
| Trigger exists | Proclisis |

Example:

- Não me viu. ✓
- Quem te chamou? ✓

because **não**, **quem**, etc. trigger proclisis.

---

In spoken Brazilian Portuguese, the trigger system is much weaker.

People simply say:

- Me chamou.
- Te vi.
- Me conta.
- Me dá.
- Me explica.

even without any traditional trigger.

---

# Imperatives Are Only One Example

Many learners first notice:

| BP | EP |
|------|------|
| Me dá. | Dá-me. |
| Me escuta. | Escuta-me. |
| Me olha. | Olha-me. |

But this is merely a visible symptom of a much larger shift.

The same thing occurs in:

### Declarative sentences

- Ele me viu.
- Eu te amo.
- Ela me chamou.

### Reflexive verbs

- Eu me levanto.
- Ela se sentou.
- Nós nos conhecemos.

### Subordinate clauses

- Acho que ele me viu.
- Espero que ela me ligue.

---

# What About Spanish and Italian?

Spanish and Italian still preserve the older Romance imperative pattern:

| Meaning | Spanish | Italian |
|----------|----------|----------|
| Kiss me! | Bésame | Baciami |
| Look at me! | Mírame | Guardami |
| Listen to me! | Escúchame | Ascoltami |

Affirmative imperatives generally require:

> Verb + Pronoun

BP is the outlier because it comfortably allows:

> Me beija!  
> Me olha!  
> Me escuta!

---

# Another Important BP Development

BP increasingly treats these forms as ordinary pronouns rather than true clitics.

For example:

Traditional object pronouns:

- o
- a
- os
- as

are often avoided in speech.

Instead Brazilians commonly say:

- Eu vi ele.
- Eu vi ela.

rather than:

- Eu o vi.
- Eu a vi.

This pushes BP even further away from the classical Romance clitic system.

---

# Practical Rule for Learners

If your goal is natural contemporary Brazilian Portuguese:

### Default

Use:

> me, te, se, nos, lhe(s) BEFORE the verb

Examples:

- Me chama.
- Te vi.
- Me explica.
- Ela me ama.
- Nós nos conhecemos.

### Understand but don't prioritize

- chama-me
- viu-me
- disse-lhe
- levantou-se

These are grammatical and common in writing, formal speech, literature, news, legal texts, and in Portugal, but they are not the default pattern of everyday Brazilian conversation.

# One-Sentence Summary

The most important thing to remember is:

> **European Portuguese still operates largely as a clitic-placement language, while Brazilian Portuguese has evolved toward a much more fixed "Subject → Pronoun → Verb" structure, making preverbal object pronouns (me, te, se, nos, lhe) the normal choice in everyday speech, including many contexts where traditional Portuguese grammar would require enclisis.**

However, there is an important nuance:

> **Many of these verbs do not merely "take an indirect object"; they conceptualize the EXPERIENCER as an indirect object (or dative-like argument).**

That's why the sentence structure often feels "backwards" to English speakers.

---

# The Mental Model

Instead of:

> X feels Y

think:

> X causes Y to be felt by Z

or

> X affects Z

where Z appears as:

- me
- te
- lhe
- nos
- lhes

which historically are indirect-object pronouns.

# Example 1: encantar

English:

> The movie fascinates me.

Portuguese:

> O filme me encanta.

Structure:

| Role | Portuguese |
|---------|---------|
| Cause | O filme |
| Experiencer | me |
| Verb | encanta |

Literally:

> The movie enchants me.

The experiencer is expressed through the clitic pronoun.

---

# Example 2: interessar

English speakers want:

> I am interested in the topic.

Portuguese often conceptualizes:

> The topic interests me.

> O assunto me interessa.

Structure:

| Role | Portuguese |
|---------|---------|
| Cause | O assunto |
| Experiencer | me |
| Verb | interessa |

# Example 3: faltar

This one is extremely revealing.

English:

> Two students are missing.

Portuguese:

> Faltam dois alunos.

Literally:

> Are lacking two students.

The subject is:

> dois alunos

not some hidden "there".

Portuguese keeps the thing that exists / lacks / remains as the grammatical subject.

---

# Example 4: doer

English:

> My head hurts.

Portuguese:

> Me dói a cabeça.

Literally:

> To me hurts the head.

Structure:

| Role | Portuguese |
|---------|---------|
| Experiencer | me |
| Verb | dói |
| Cause/subject | a cabeça |

This is very close to Spanish:

> Me duele la cabeza.

Notice the pronouns:

- nos
- lhe
- lhes

These are exactly the pronouns that BP strongly prefers before the verb:

- Me dói a cabeça.
- Me interessa.
- Me agrada.
- Me surpreende.

This is where the BP proclisis preference becomes highly visible.

More and more:

> Subject → Pronoun → Verb

- Isso me agrada.

# A Practical Rule

When you encounter verbs expressing:

- liking
- interest
- pain
- surprise
- annoyance
- suitability
- remaining/lacking

try asking:

> "What is causing the feeling?"

That thing is often the grammatical **subject**.

Then ask:

> "Who experiences it?"

That person is often expressed with:

- me
- te
- lhe
- nos
- lhes

historically the **indirect-object series**.

So:

> A música me fascina.

is best understood as:

**Subject:** a música  
**Experiencer (dative-like/indirect-object):** me  
**Verb:** fascina

rather than trying to force the English pattern:

> "I am fascinated by the music."

This single mental model explains a surprisingly large portion of everyday Brazilian Portuguese.

# The Deep Generalization

Historically, all four languages had:

| Function | Portuguese | Spanish | Italian |
|----------|----------|----------|----------|
| 3rd-person direct object | o/a | lo/la | lo/la |
| 3rd-person indirect object | lhe | le | gli |

| Language | Direct object clitic | Indirect object clitic |
|----------|----------|----------|
| PT-BR | often replaced by **ele/ela** | often replaced by **para ele/ela** |
| PT-EU | preserved | preserved |
| Spanish | preserved | preserved |
| Italian | preserved | preserved |

> **BP has weakened both third-person direct-object clitics (o/a) and third-person indirect-object clitics (lhe), often replacing them with full pronouns (ele/ela) or prepositional phrases (para ele/ela). EP, Spanish, and Italian still rely heavily on the traditional clitic system.**

This is arguably a bigger difference than the proclisis vs enclisis issue itself.

Yes — **clitics are exactly what we've been talking about.**

In Portuguese grammar, words such as:

- me
- te
- se
- nos
- lhe(s)
- o(s)
- a(s)

are traditionally called **clitic pronouns** (*pronomes clíticos*).

The terms:

- **proclisis** = clitic before the verb → **me viu**
- **enclisis** = clitic after the verb → **viu-me**
- **mesoclisis** = clitic inside the verb → **ver-me-á**

all describe the **position of clitics** relative to the verb.

The deeper point is:

> **Modern Brazilian Portuguese is gradually treating many of these clitics less like clitics and more like ordinary pronouns.**

For example:

| Classical clitic system | Modern BP tendency |
|----------|----------|
| Vi-o | Vi ele |
| Disse-lhe | Disse para ele |
| Chama-o! | Chama ele! |

So when people say:

> "BP has weakened the clitic system"

they are talking about exactly the phenomena we've been discussing.

# Dimension A — Clitic Type (What the clitic means)

| Type | Example |
|--------|--------|
| Direct Object | me, te, o, a |
| Indirect Object | me, te, lhe |
| Reflexive | me, te, se, nos |
| Reciprocal | nos, se |
| Impersonal/Passive se | se |

Examples:

- **Ele me viu.** (direct object)
- **Ele me disse.** (indirect object)
- **Eu me levantei.** (reflexive)
- **Nós nos conhecemos.** (reciprocal)
- **Vende-se casa.** (passive se)
- **Precisa-se de ajuda.** (impersonal se)

---

# Dimension B — Clitic Position (Your D2/D4 discussion)

| Position | Example |
|--------|--------|
| Proclisis | me viu |
| Enclisis | viu-me |
| Mesoclisis | ver-me-á |

This dimension is independent of the previous one.

For example, the same reflexive clitic can appear in different positions:

| Type | Proclisis | Enclisis |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Reflexive | se levantou | levantou-se |

---

# Dimension C — BP Speech vs Writing

| Register | Typical Pattern |
|--------|--------|
| Speech | proclisis, ele/ela, para ele |
| Writing | more clitics, more enclisis |

Examples:

| Speech | Formal |
|--------|--------|
| vi ele | vi-o |
| disse para ele | disse-lhe |
| chama ele | chama-o |

---

# Where Your D1–D4 Fit

| Your Dimension | Linguistic Dimension |
|--------|--------|
| D1 Direct vs Indirect Object | Clitic Type |
| D2 Finite vs Imperative | Verb Environment |
| D3 Third vs Non-Third Person | Pronoun Category |
| D4 Speech vs Writing | Register |

None of these is the same thing as "clitic".

They are factors that influence:

> **whether a clitic is used at all, and if used, where it is placed.**

---

# The Special Case of "se"

This is probably what makes the topic feel confusing.

The same word **se** can belong to several different subsets:

| Function | Example |
|--------|--------|
| Reflexive | Ela se levantou. |
| Reciprocal | Eles se conhecem. |
| Passive | Vende-se casa. |
| Impersonal | Precisa-se de ajuda. |

All are clitics.

So:

> **Reflexive** and **impersonal/passive se** are subtypes of clitic usage.

But:

> **Direct vs indirect object, finite vs imperative, third vs non-third, speech vs writing** are separate dimensions that affect how those clitics behave.

That's the clean conceptual hierarchy.

## 5. Reflexive / Pronominal Verbs

From your Excel notes, these fall into three useful groups:

| Type | Meaning | Example | Important point |
|---|---|---|---|
| **True reflexive** | subject acts on itself | **Ele se cortou.** | “He cut himself.” |
| **Change of state / movement** | subject enters a state | **Ela se levantou.** | Not always literally “herself” |
| **Inherently pronominal** | verb normally needs the clitic | **Eu me arrependi.** | You usually cannot drop **se/me/te** |

Examples:

| Verb | Natural BP |
|---|---|
| levantar-se | **Ela se levantou cedo.** |
| sentar-se | **Ele se sentou.** |
| arrepender-se | **Eu me arrependi.** |
| queixar-se | **Ela se queixou da dor.** |
| orgulhar-se | **Eu me orgulho de você.** |
| divertir-se | **Nós nos divertimos.** |
| preocupar-se | **Não se preocupe.** |

---

## 6. Impersonal / Passive **se**

| Type | Structure | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Impersonal se** | verb usually 3rd singular | **Precisa-se de ajuda.** | Help is needed / one needs help |
| **Impersonal se** | general subject | **Fala-se português no Brasil.** | Portuguese is spoken in Brazil |
| **Passive se** | verb agrees with noun | **Vendem-se carros.** | Cars are sold |
| **Passive se** | plural agreement | **Alugam-se apartamentos.** | Apartments are rented |

Important distinction:

| Form | Analysis |
|---|---|
| **Precisa-se de ajuda.** | impersonal **se**; no clear subject |
| **Vendem-se carros.** | passive **se**; **carros** behaves like subject |

## 7. Where Enclisis Survives Strongly in PT-BR

| Context | Common Form | Why |
|---|---|---|
| manuals / websites | **Cadastre-se.** | institutional command style |
| signs / instructions | **Identifique-se.** | fixed formal pattern |
| formal commands | **Faça-o.** | careful writing |
| legal/admin text | **Encaminhe-o ao setor.** | bureaucratic style |
| dictionary infinitives | **arrepender-se** | citation form |
| very formal style | **Disse-lhe / viu-me** | literary or conservative |

# Final Learner Rules

| Rule | Memorize |
|---|---|
| **R1** | For **me, te, nos**, use **pronoun + verb**: **Te vi**, **Me chama**, **Nos encontramos**. |
| **R2** | For 3rd-person direct object in speech, use **ele/ela**: **Vi ele**, **Chama ela**. |
| **R3** | For 3rd-person indirect object in speech, use **para ele/ela**: **Diz para ele**, **Entrega para ela**. |
| **R4** | For formal writing, expect more **o/a/lhe**: **Eu o vi**, **Diga-lhe**. |
| **R5** | Reflexive/pronominal verbs keep the clitic: **me arrependo**, **se levantou**, **nos divertimos**. |
| **R6** | **se** has many jobs: reflexive, reciprocal, passive, impersonal. Do not treat every **se** as “himself/herself.” |
| **R7** | Enclisis in Brazil survives mostly in formal/institutional style: **Cadastre-se**, **Identifique-se**, **Faça-o**. |

The deepest summary:

> **Modern spoken PT-BR keeps me/te/se/nos before the verb, often replaces 3rd-person clitics with ele/ela or para ele/ela, but still preserves se strongly in reflexive, pronominal, passive, and institutional-command patterns.**
